# DNMS: A Guide for the CEO

**DNMS (Digitally Next Management System)** · Prepared October 2026

This guide explains what DNMS does, how each part works, and where to look when you want an answer. You don't need to read it in one go. Sections 1 to 4 give you the big picture in about ten minutes. Section 5 is a reference you can come back to.

---

## 1. DNMS in one minute

DNMS is the one place where Digitally Next runs. It holds three things:

- **Our people.** Who works here and who reports to whom, plus attendance, leave, work from home, salaries, performance reviews, and joining and leaving.
- **Our client work.** Every client and every project: what each team promised this week, who is doing it, and whether it was delivered.
- **Company life.** Chat, announcements, photos, policies and everyday tools.

Everyone in the company uses the same website. **What each person sees depends on their role.** An employee sees only their own leave, payslips and projects. HR sees HR tools. **You are an Admin, so you can see and do everything.**

**Web address:** https://dnms.digitallynext.com

## 3. How the pieces connect

Most of DNMS follows two stories: an employee's time at the company, and a client project.

### The life of an employee

1. **Joining.** HR adds the person. DNMS creates their onboarding checklist automatically, plus a **15-day scorecard** for their first 15 working days.
2. **Every day.** They punch in and out on the office attendance machine. Their attendance appears in DNMS on its own; nobody types it in.
3. **Time off.** They apply for leave or work from home in DNMS. Their manager or HR approves it, emails go out, and their balance updates.
4. **Every month.** HR generates payroll. Each person's payslip appears in their account, and they get an email when it is marked paid.
5. **Reviews.** For each review period, the employee rates themselves and their manager rates them. The score decides the outcome: promotion, increment, review, or improvement plan.
6. **Leaving.** They submit a resignation from their profile. Once it's approved, an exit checklist starts. Their manager, Finance and IT/Admin each sign off. HR then completes the exit, which closes the account.

### The life of a client project

1. **Client.** A client record is created with an **Account Manager**, the person who owns that relationship.
2. **Project.** A project is created for the client. It automatically gets **11 standard teams**: AM, Web, Design, Video, Content, SMO, SEO, Performance, PR, Alliances & Partnerships, and Admin. The Account Manager puts people on them.
3. **Kick-off.** The Account Manager works through the **Pre Campaign Kick Start** checklist (10 must-haves) and fills in the client's **Brand** brief and **Goals**.
4. **Every week.** The Account Manager plans a **deliverable**: what each team owes the client that week (for example, 4 blogs from Content and 2 reels from Video).
5. **The work.** Team members plan their days in a weekly sheet and move tasks through statuses. Time is counted while a task is marked **In Progress**.
6. **Checking.** Finished items are logged with proof (a link or a file). The team's manager checks each item, and then the Account Manager **accepts** it or **sends it back** with a reason.
7. **Reporting.** The **Progress** page shows what's done, open and overdue, and produces ready-made reports. Clients can sign in to a **client portal** and see only what we allow.

## 5. Module by module

Each module below covers **what it is**, **how it works**, and **what's worth your attention**.

### 5.1 Dashboard

**What it is:** The home page you land on after signing in.

**How it works:** Because you can see the whole company, your Dashboard shows:
- **Total Employees, New This Month, Total Documents** and unread notifications
- **Department Headcount** and **Employee Status** (Active, On Leave, Resigned and so on)
- **Recent Joiners**, and shortcuts to add an employee, upload a document or view the audit log
- At the bottom: **recent announcements, event photos and upcoming birthdays**, where you can click **Send wishes**

Regular employees see their own day instead: tasks due today, overdue work, attendance, leave left, their latest payslip and their latest rating.

### PEOPLE

### 5.2 Employee Directory

**What it is:** The list of everyone in the company. Click any name to see their full profile.

**How it works:**
- HR adds a new person with a 5-step form: personal info, employment, documents, address and emergency contact, then review.
- Each profile holds their job details, documents, role, leave, attendance and history.
- **Deactivate** closes a person's access but **keeps all their records**. For people who resign, HR uses Exit Clearance instead, which closes the account at the right moment.
- The **Roles** tab on a profile decides what that person can see and do in DNMS (see section 7).

**Related lists** (under HRMS › Employees):
- **Departments:** how the company is organised, up to three levels deep (department, sub-department, sub-sub-department).
- **Designations:** job titles.
- **Job Roles:** the role a person plays within their department.

### 5.3 Organisation Chart

**What it is:** A picture of who reports to whom, from the top down. Every active employee is a card, with lines joining managers to their team. You can fold teams open or shut and zoom in or out. It updates on its own whenever HR changes who someone reports to.

### 5.4 Onboarding

**What it is:** A checklist for every new joiner, from the week before they start to the end of their first month.

**How it works:**
- The checklist **starts automatically** as soon as HR adds the employee.
- It follows the company's onboarding steps, from "To-do Before Joining" to "First Month Milestones". Each item has an owner (HR, or the manager) and a due date, and shows Pending, Overdue or Done.
- **15-day scorecard:** for each of the first 15 working days, the manager scores job understanding, communication and learning, and HR scores discipline, attendance, culture fit and adaptability (1 to 5). DNMS works out the averages. At the end, HR records a recommendation: *Continue as planned*, *Continue with improvement areas*, or *Review required*.

**Worth your attention:** The scorecard is the earliest signal of whether a new hire is working out.

### 5.5 Resignations

**What it is:** Where resignations arrive and get decided.

**How it works:**
1. The employee applies from **My Profile › Apply Resignation**, giving a reason and the last working day they want.
2. Their manager and HR are notified. The red number in the sidebar shows how many are waiting.
3. **Approve:** the notice period starts and the exit checklist is created. The person keeps full access until their last day so they can hand over work.
4. **Decline:** the employee is told (with an optional note) and carries on as before.

While a resignation is still pending, the employee can withdraw it.

### 5.6 Exit Clearance

**What it is:** A checklist that makes sure nothing is left pending when someone leaves.

**How it works:**
- It starts **automatically** when a resignation is approved.
- The items already have owners. HR handles HR items. The person leaving prepares the handover document. Their manager runs the handover meeting.
- **Clearances** are sign-offs from the **Manager**, **Finance** and **IT/Admin**, for example confirming the laptop was returned. Each owner signs from their own **Waiting on you** page.
- When every required clearance is signed, HR clicks **Complete exit**. The person is marked Resigned, **their account is closed**, they are **removed from all project teams**, and a relieving confirmation is emailed to them.

**Worth your attention:** The page shows who is serving notice, who leaves within 7 days, and whose exit is **blocked** on a missing sign-off.

### TIME: ATTENDANCE, HOLIDAYS, LEAVE, WFH

### 5.7 Attendance

**What it is:** A record of when each person came in and left.

**How it works:**
- Nobody checks in on DNMS. People **punch on the Hikvision attendance machines** in the office, and the punches reach DNMS on their own.
- **Attendance Directory** shows who is in today, any date range, or one person's whole month as a colour-coded calendar (present, leave, WFH, holiday and so on).
- Employees **can't edit** their own attendance. If someone forgot to punch or the machine got it wrong, HR corrects it.
- **Devices** is where the attendance machines are connected to DNMS.

### 5.8 Calendar and Holidays

**What it is:** The company holiday list and everyone's birthdays.

**How it works:**
- HR adds the year's holidays.
- **Floating holidays** are optional days off chosen from a list HR sets up. Each person can pick **up to 3 a year**. The manager gives an opinion first and HR makes the final call.

### 5.9 Leave

**What it is:** Leave balances, leave requests and the leave policy.

**How it works:**
- Employees see a card for each leave type showing how many days they have left, and apply from **My Leave**.
- A request goes to **HR and the employee's own manager**. **Whoever acts first decides it.**
- **Leave requests from HR staff go to an Admin** for the decision, so some may come to you.
- Each approval or rejection sends the employee an email, and their balance updates on its own.
- **Leave Directory** (HRMS › Leave) has three tabs: **Requests** (everyone's), **On Leave** (who is off, filterable by dates) and **Balances** (days left for each person).
- **Leave Types & Policy** sets how many days of each leave type a person gets per year, by employment type (Full Time, Part Time, Contract, Intern). Days are given either a share each month or all at once.

**Rules DNMS enforces automatically** (the form stops the request and explains why):
- **Sandwich rule:** weekends that fall between leave days count as leave.
- **Casual Leave:** apply at least 2 days ahead, and take no more than 2 days a month.
- **Short Leave:** counts as half a day, 2 per month.
- **Earned Leave:** apply 60 days ahead, 3 to 7 days at a time, and no more than 7 days in each half of the year. It becomes available 6 months after probation ends.
- Casual or Sick Leave can't be attached to Earned Leave.
- Paid leave starts after probation (after 6 months of service for contract staff).
- No leave can be applied during the notice period.

### 5.10 Work From Home (WFH)

**What it is:** WFH requests and approvals.

**How it works:**
- **During probation and the first 6 months after it:** only **Emergency** WFH is allowed.
- **After that:** **1 regular WFH day per month**, plus emergency requests, which don't use up that day.
- One request covers at most 14 days. Weekends, holidays and days already on leave can't be picked.
- The request emails the manager, with HR copied. **The manager or HR approves**, and the first decision is final. A rejection must give a reason, which the employee sees.
- **HRMS › Work From Home** shows every request in the company.

### MONEY AND THINGS

### 5.11 Payroll

**What it is:** Salaries and monthly payslips.

**How it works:**
1. **Salary Structures:** HR sets each person's monthly salary. DNMS splits it into Basic, HRA, Transport, Medical, Phone and Special allowance using the standard percentages, which can be adjusted.
2. **Generate Payroll:** once a month, HR clicks one button and DNMS creates a **Draft** payslip for every active employee with a salary structure. It's safe to run again, because existing payslips are skipped.
3. **Check:** HR can add overtime or other deductions to a Draft payslip. Pay is worked out per day (monthly salary ÷ 30). **Unpaid days (LOP)** come from attendance: an absent day that isn't covered by approved paid leave is unpaid. Weekends, holidays and approved floating holidays are paid.
4. **Statuses:** each payslip moves through **Draft → Processing → Approved → Paid**. When it is marked **Paid**, the employee gets an email with the amount.

Employees see only their own payslips, in **My Payslips**, and can save them as PDF.

**Worth your attention:** The Payroll Directory cards show **Total Payroll** and **Net Payable** for any month.

### 5.12 Stock Register

**What it is:** A record of what HR buys (diaries, T-shirts, ID card holders and so on), who each item was given to, and how much is left. HR can restock items, record returns, and import or export the register as Excel.

### PERFORMANCE AND HIRING

### 5.13 Performance

**What it is:** Performance reviews (called "evaluations") and the KPIs each person is scored on.

**How it works:**
- **KPI Profiles:** HR sets up the list of items each person is scored on. A standard company list can be loaded and then adjusted.
- **Evaluations:** HR clicks **Generate Evaluations**, which creates a scorecard for everyone with a KPI profile and notifies them and their managers.
- The **employee rates themselves** and the **manager rates them separately**, each line from 1 to 5. **Role performance (KPIs) counts for 60%** and **workplace discipline and execution for 40%**.
- **The final score is the manager's score.** The self-rating sits beside it for comparison.
- An optional **Project Controller** can add a third review. It's shown alongside but doesn't change the score.

**What the score means:**
| Score | Rating | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 95% and above | Exceptional Performer | Fast-track promotion within 6 months |
| 90 to 94% | Outstanding Performer | Eligible for increment and promotion |
| 85 to 89% | Strong Performer | Eligible for increment only |
| 75 to 84% | Developing Performer | No increment, 1-month review |
| 65 to 74% | Needs Improvement | 2-week improvement plan, exit if no improvement |
| Below 65% | Unsatisfactory | 1-week improvement plan, exit if no improvement |

### 5.14 Recruitment

**What it is:** Our public careers site, job applications and employee referrals.

**How it works:**
- **Careers:** controls what candidates see on the public careers website. Openings are organised as groups → sub-departments → roles → openings. Each item is **Live** (visible) or **Draft** (hidden while being prepared).
- **Applications:** everyone who applied through the careers site, with their CV. HR moves each candidate through **New → In review → Shortlisted → Hired or Rejected**, and can keep internal notes the candidate never sees.
- **Referrals:** candidates put forward by our employees. If the person is hired and **completes one year**, the employee who referred them earns a reward (a share of the new hire's monthly salary). DNMS tracks when it falls due. Marking it paid only records the payment; it doesn't send money.

### 5.15 Analytics

**What it is:** A one-page summary of the whole company.

**How it works:** Cards show Total Employees, Pending Leaves, Attendance This Month and Last Payroll Net. Charts show monthly hires (last 6 months), headcount by department, employee status, and the hiring pipeline. Point at any bar to see the exact number.

*Two things to read carefully:* "Attendance This Month" counts **person-days**, not people (10 people present for 5 days shows 50). The hiring pipeline chart counts internal job postings, not careers-site applications, so it can show 0 while the Applications page has candidates.

### CLIENT WORK

### 5.16 Projects

**What it is:** Every client project. As an Admin you see all of them; others see only the projects they are on.

**How it works:**
- The list shows each project's code, client, services, status, **Account Manager**, tasks and people. It can be exported to Excel.
- **The Account Manager runs the project.** They can manage everything on their own project, even without admin rights.
- Every project has the **same 11 teams**. Teams are never added or renamed; only the people on them change. Each team can have a **team manager**.
- Projects aren't deleted. When the work ends, the status is set to **Completed** or **Cancelled**.
- Each service on a project (for example SEO or social media) has an **owner** and its own monthly calendar.

**Inside a project, the tabs are:**
| Tab | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | Summary: Account Manager, project code, onboarding date, services and their owners, goal progress. Budget is visible to Admins and the Account Manager only. |
| **Brand** | The client's brand brief, digital targets, content themes, strategy and brand guidelines. AI can help write the brief. |
| **Goals** | What the project is meant to achieve, broken into sub-goals and targets, with progress. |
| **Calendars** | Monthly spreadsheets for each service, such as a content calendar. Shows what each team owes this month. Can be downloaded as Excel or CSV, and the Account Manager can share a calendar with the client. |
| **Deliverables** | The weekly promise to the client: what each team will deliver, with proof and sign-off (see 5.17). |
| **Repository** | The project's files and links, kept in the project's Google Drive folder and shared with the team. Backed up every night. |
| **Analytics** | The client's Meta (Facebook and Instagram) ad results. |
| **SEO** | How the client's websites are doing in Google search, from Google Search Console. |
| **Teams** | The 11 teams and the people on each. |
| **Tasks** | A weekly sheet of the whole project team's planned work against what they actually did, day by day. |
| **Requirements** | What the team is waiting for, usually from the client: a document, a login, an approval. Shows how many are still open. |
| **Chats** | Group conversations for everyone on the project, one per topic, like WhatsApp groups. |
| **Activity** | The history of what happened on the project, and who did it. |
| **Pre Campaign Kick Start** | A checklist of 10 must-haves (31 items) to complete before a campaign starts. Account Manager and Admins only. |
| **Passwords** | The client's logins and keys, kept safely. Account Manager and Admins only. |
| **Monitoring** | Alerts when the client's website goes down, and reminders before a domain, SSL certificate, hosting plan or licence expires. |
| **Mailer** | Email campaigns sent from the client's own email account to a list kept on the project. |

### 5.17 Deliverables: how client work gets checked

This is how we make sure what we promise the client actually gets delivered.

1. **Plan:** the Account Manager plans a deliverable for a week, for example "Content: 4 blogs, Video: 2 reels, Design: 6 banners".
2. **Assign:** the team manager gives each item to a team member, or a member picks it up themselves.
3. **Log work:** the member records progress with **proof** (a link or a file), then marks the item **Made**.
4. **First check:** the team's manager checks the work.
5. **Final yes:** the Account Manager marks it **Accepted**, or **sends it back** with a reason. If it's sent back, the item shows "rev 1" so everyone can see it took two attempts.

Every change keeps a history of who did it and why. Clients can see the plan and add requests in the portal, but **only our team can accept or send back work.**

### 5.18 My Work and Tasks

**What it is:** Each person's own work across all their projects, in a weekly **plan vs. actual** sheet, the same shape as the Excel sheet the team used before.

**How it works:**
- People plan their week, then fill in what they actually did and the hours spent.
- Tasks move through statuses (Open, In Progress, On hold, Done and so on). **Time is only counted while a task is In Progress**, so the hours in reports depend on people using this properly.
- Meetings and non-client work go in as **ADHOC** tasks.
- Managers can open any team member's work from a picker at the top. As an Admin, you can open anyone's.

### 5.19 Progress and Progress Reports

**What it is:** The best single page for judging whether client work is on track.

**How it works:**
- It counts **deliverables** (the items promised to clients), not individual tasks.
- Four boxes at the top: **To do, Completed, Overdue now, Sent back.** Click any box to see the list behind the number.
- Narrow it by **date range, project, team or person**. As an Admin you see **everything in the company**.
- **Export** produces an Excel, Word or PowerPoint file of what's on screen, with an optional AI-written summary and talking points.
- **Progress report** produces a ready-to-present weekly or monthly report (PowerPoint, PDF or Word) for a person or a whole team. It covers hours by project, what was delivered, what was done each day, what's still open, and attendance alongside.

**Worth your attention:** For a team review meeting, pick the team and the week, then click **Progress report › PowerPoint**.

### 5.20 Clients and the Client Portal

**What it is:** A record of every client company, its projects and its people. It also controls what clients can see.

**How it works:**
- Each client has a status (**Prospect, Active, Inactive**), an Account Manager, contact details and its projects.
- **Contacts** are people at the client company who can sign in to the **client portal**. For each contact, we choose **which projects and which sections** they see (for example, the content plan or email campaigns). Anything not ticked stays hidden.
- DNMS emails the contact their password; nobody on our side sees it. Access can be paused per project, or switched off entirely.
- The **Activity** tab shows what each client contact did in the portal and when.

## 6. AI inside DNMS

AI is built into several places:

- **Ask DNMS** (top bar): ask questions in plain English, like *"Who is away this week?"*, *"What's overdue?"* or *"What's waiting for my approval?"*. It sees only what you're allowed to see, and **never reveals salaries or personal details**.
- **Improve with AI:** on leave and WFH requests, approval letters and the client brand brief, it rewrites text more clearly. You can always undo it.
- **AI summaries:** Progress exports can include a written summary and talking points.
- **AI Connections:** you can connect **Claude or ChatGPT** to DNMS and work from there. For example, ask *"Summarise this week's overdue deliverables"* or *"Who is on leave next week?"*. The AI gets **exactly your own permissions, nothing more**. You can disconnect it at any time, and Admins can see and cut off anyone's connection, for example when someone leaves.

## 7. Who can see what

**Company-wide roles** (one per person):
| Role | In short |
|---|---|
| **Admin** | Everything, including the Admin pages. |
| **HR Manager** | All HR work: employees, attendance, leave, payroll, hiring, onboarding and exits. Also the Audit Log and Email Templates, and managing projects. |
| **HR Employee** | Day-to-day HR, such as approving leave and WFH and helping with hiring. Can't run payroll or complete exits. |
| **Employee** | Their own leave, attendance, payslips, performance and projects. |

Custom roles can also be created, for example "Project Coordinator", with only the permissions that job needs.

**Positions that come from the work, not from a role:**
- **Reporting manager:** approves their direct reports' leave, WFH and floating holidays, and rates them in reviews.
- **Account Manager:** runs everything on their own projects, including passwords and budget.
- **Team manager:** runs their team within a project and gives the first check on deliverables.
- **Client portal contact:** someone at a client company who sees only the projects and sections we allow.

## 8. Be careful with these

As an Admin, nothing is off-limits to you. A few actions can't be undone, or affect other people straight away:

- **Leave and WFH decisions are final.** There's no undo once a request is approved or rejected.
- **Completing an exit** closes the person's account and removes them from every project team.
- **Changing someone's role** changes what they can see within about 15 minutes. Give the **Admin** role sparingly.
- **Marking payroll Paid** emails each employee their amount.
- **Sync balances** (Leave policy) resets everyone's yearly leave entitlement to the new policy. Days already used are kept.
- **Generate Evaluations** notifies every employee and manager at once.
- **The Passwords tab** in a project holds client logins. Treat it like a safe.
- **Deactivate, don't delete.** Deactivating a person keeps all their records.

## 9. A suggested first week

About 15 minutes a day:

| Day | What to do |
|---|---|
| **1** | Sign in, set your password, add DNMS to your phone's home screen. Look around the Dashboard. Ask **Ask DNMS** a question. |
| **2** | **People:** open the Employee Directory and the Organisation Chart. Open two or three profiles. |

## 10. Words you'll see

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Account Manager (AM)** | The person who owns a client relationship and runs that client's projects. |
| **Deliverable** | One week's (or one period's) promised work for a client, broken into items per team. |
| **Made / Accepted / Sent back** | A deliverable item that is finished / approved by the Account Manager / returned for changes. |
| **Task** | A single piece of work in someone's weekly plan. |
| **ADHOC task** | Work with no client, such as internal meetings. |
| **Requirement** | Something the team is waiting for, usually from the client. |
| **Service owner** | The person responsible for one service (such as SEO) on a project. |
| **Client portal** | The part of DNMS clients sign in to. They see only what we allow. |
| **Floating holiday** | An optional holiday picked from HR's list (up to 3 a year). |
| **Sandwich rule** | Weekends between leave days count as leave. |
| **LOP** | Loss of pay: unpaid days deducted from salary. |
| **Emergency WFH** | Urgent work-from-home that doesn't use up the monthly WFH day. |
| **KPI profile** | The list of items a person is scored on in reviews. |
| **Evaluation** | A review scorecard for one person for one period. |
| **15-day scorecard** | A daily score for a new joiner's first 15 working days. |
| **Exit clearance** | Sign-offs needed (Manager, Finance, IT/Admin) before someone is relieved. |
| **Role / Permission** | A permission is one thing a person can do. A role is a set of permissions. |
| **Audit Log** | The history of important changes in DNMS. |
